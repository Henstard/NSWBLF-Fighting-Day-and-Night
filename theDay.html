The Heat of Day

In the heat of the day, long hours upon builder labourers were incredibly toiling. This was made especially worse, due to the lack of ammenities on the site. There were no sheds to hide from the heat, no washrooms to cools and wash off sweat, and you were lucky if you had a hook to hang your clothes on. Additionally, many still engaged in a practice known as "riding the hook", where a worker would ride a crane hook all the way to the top in order to scale buildings.

Thus, the BLF took the effort to place bans on work sites that were not up to scratch, worker would walk off when the temperature became to hot, amongst other measures. When one member, Tom Hogan, found there to be no facilties on his work site, he ended up hurling an air compressor down an excavation hole, to find the next day that the site was fitted with facilities. Moreover, the union pushed against practices such as riding the hook, and pushed for sites to require two "dogmen" to operate a crane. Through such efforts, the union ultimately conquered the difficulties of the day.

Preserving the Day's Green

As mentioned, the NSWBLF pioneered a phenonmenon known as the "Green Ban". Developers, whom otherwise gave little care to environmental sites such as Centennial Park and Kelly's Bush, felt that they were entitled to develop the bush as they so pleased. However, as resident activist groups requested the help of the NSWBLF, BLF union members would refuse to work on sites that had Green Bans placed on them. The effect of this, was the preservation of much of the natural environment of Sydney, that we stil can see today. These bans also stretched to the built environment as well, saving areas such as The Rocks and Wolloomolloo.

The "Green Ban" is also the etymological origin of virtually every Green party on Earth. Starting with the leader of the German Greens party taking inspiration from the NSWBLF green bans, it eventually spread across Europe and back into Australia, even having much influence in "green" terminology terms in general. It remains one of the most unique utilisations of union power Australia (and the world) has ever seen.

Sunset

But with the militant tactics of the NSWBLF, making enemies was inevitable. With $3 billion worth of unethical construction projects being held up, the developers started getting desperate. They therefore got behind the Federal Secretary of the union, Norm Gallagher, to undertake a coup d'etat of the NSWBLF's leadership. This was motivated by the fact that the developers and the State Government threatened the BLF with deregistration, and offered reregistration of the union in exchange for the NSWBLF branch being eliminated. Starting in 1974 and ending in 1975, Gallagher crushed the NSWBLF branch and ended the democratic dream that Mundey, Owens and Pringle had so long fought for.

As a consequence, Gallagher became the face of the BLF for the years to come. This gave perfect justification for Australia to register the BLF in 1986, following many of Gallagher's corrupt dealings with developers, as to build his own beachhouse. So the sun set twice on the BLF, once in 1975, and once again in 1986.
