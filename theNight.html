The Hoist and the Bars

Around the 1940s and 50s, the NSWBLF was under control of a gangster-like leader known as Fred Thomas. Fred Thomas was well known for running the branch through violence, intimidation and corruption, whilst generally opposing strikes and other militant action for improved wages or conditions on the worksite. Opposition was inevitable, but it had to come under the dead of night.

A group known as the "Rank and File" started to have nighttime after-hour meetings across Sydney. In places like Town Hall, Hyde Park or The Grand Hotel, they discussed improvements needed for work conditions, wet weather pay, and how to develop a rank-and-file force against the tyranny of the Thomas leadership. Through these meetings, and the distribution the "Hoist" newspaper, a powerful force arose from the darkness to gradually push out the corrupt elements of the leadership, with the Rank and File Committee member Nick McNamara being elected as General Secretary in 1961. The cover of night had given the workers the ability to grow their numbers, and the victory of this election proved to be the fruits of their labour for the series of years to come.

The Vigilantes

When the NSWBLF had placed a "ban" (Green Ban/Black Ban) on an operating site, it meant that the site had been designated to not be developed upon. The developers sought to break these bans through the hiring of non-union labour, known colloquially as "scab" labour. In order to maintain whatever ban the union had placed upon a particular site, the NSWBLF would send out vigilante squads at the dark of night, to destroy the any labour the "scabs" had built. Once the sun rose in the morning, the developers would find any labour the "scabs" had built recently destroyed, much to their frustration.

The NSW government at the time complained greatly about these actions, and instigated rumours about the non-union workers being attacked by union members. In response, NSWBLF Secretary Jack Mundey asked for the government to provide one instance of a worker being physically attacked by the NSWBLF. They failed to return one incident.

Victoria Street: Night Squatters

One of the most brutal battles of the NSWBLF "Green Bans", was the Battle for Victoria Street. The developer planning to demolish and change much of Victoria Street, was a man by the name of Frank Theeman, was exceedingly brutal in his methods to force the original working-class residents of Victoria Street out. His links with criminal elements, meant he could send thugs to throw bricks at and kick the doors down of tenants unwilling to leave. He also was not unwilling to kidnap those standing in opposition to him.

To maintain the ban, the NSWBLF thus had to conquer the night, through the deployment of squatters. Tenants who had been forced from their homes, had BLF squatters live in their properties overnight, deploying makeshift blockades against thugs and even barbed wire. In the end, whilst the physical buildings of Victoria Street were preserved, most of the residents would never see their homes again.
