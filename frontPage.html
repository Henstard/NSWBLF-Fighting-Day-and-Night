The NSWBLF: Fighting Day and Night

The New South Wales branch of the Builders Labourers Federation, at least until 1975, was perhaps one of the most unique Australian political phenonmenons. Day and night, they challenged some of the most powerful people in their contemporary society, preserved both natural and built heritage, established strong working conditions in their industry and shielded the working class and the most vulnerable groups of their society.

In this website, we are going to explore how they worked around the clock, both day and night, to build their platform, maintain their principles and hold firm their militant power. The website is divided by top and bottom. The top navigation bar details ways the BLF fought during the day, and fought for the day we have now, and the bottom navigation bar details ways the BLF fought during the night.

THE LEADERS

Joe Owens

Joe Owens served as Secretary between 1973 and 1975, but had been an engaged unions organiser in the union much earlier. His efforts as a union organiser saw him campaign heavily against dangerous industry practices, such as the "riding the hook" on cranes, where a worker would stand on a crane's hook to be hoisted up buildings.

Jack Mundey

Jack Mundey served as Secretary between 1968 and 1973. Mundey played a key role in the union's implementation of 'Green Bans', where BLF members would go on strike on sites whose preservation were requested by the local residents. Mundey pushed strongly for a limited tenure of office and democractic structures in the unions, voluntarily stepping down from power.

Bob Pringle

Bob Pringe served as the President between 1969 and 1975. Pringle was an instrumental figure in the pioneering of the Green Ban concept, through his formal recommendation to the union to apply the first Green Ban in Kelly's Bush in 1971. He additionally practice broad social activism efforts, namely assisting to establish the Aboriginal Tent Embassy in 1972 and attempting to saw down the SCG goalposts before a 1971 rubgy game South Africa was partaking in.
